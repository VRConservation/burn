> "Fire suppression is treating a sympton, rather than a cure, that's never going away." 

# Problem
1. NYT article with team installing cameras to spot wildfires. The reporter did caveat that AI could id fire and managers could decide what to do but overarching message was suppression
2. Stanford report showing billions spent on suppression and a disproportionate amount going to CA
3. Kleeman or other studies showing negative effects of Rx fire. True that woodsmoke is going to have -ve effects, but Rx fire is where and when you want it and not like megafires that happen during extreme weather events with far more negative consequences.

:::{important} Make fire to avoid fire
:class: dropdown

Explanation of the importance of prescribed fire as good fire.
:::

# Solutions
1. Cite about the low percentage of Rx fires that have gotten out of control
2. Rx fire at Caples Lake that diverted the Caldor fire
3. Early warnings for sensitive groups to avoid smoke exposure
