# Problem
Smoky the Bear's campaign of 'Only you can prevent forest fires' was extremely successful, but probably THE worst thing to happen to Western United States forests during the past two centuries. Prior to and following created nearly a century of fire supresssion in fire adapted forests that has led to a deep fire deficit and caused death and destruction of communities, wildlife, and forests across the west. It's difficult to see how to dig out, especially given the decline in funds to forest restoration and close to 30-50% loss in capacity to many federal agencies that manage the lands where forest and fires are located.

Ecological thinning with prescribed fire is one solution, but getting to the scale needed is proving difficult. But allowing some firest to burn and others not, especially in wildland areas where communities are not threatened, will have to be part of the strategy. # Solutions
Overall, we advocate for creating layered resilience as a descending staircase where each step from landscape to building scale rests on the previous layer [@donut]. The overlapping layers reinforce one another, and no single treatment layer is sufficient to mitigate wildfire. A treated forest changes how a fire arrives; a hardened home changes what happens when it does. Community planning and defensible space connect the two. Protection comes from the overlapping scales, where one layer covers what another cannot. Any gaps in coverage let the fire through. Additional solutions include:

1. **Nuanced suppresssion**. There needs to be a nuance to suppression: quick suppression in the WUI and urban areas, especially during extreme weather events but allowing other fires burn that do not threaten communities or vulnerable WUI regions.
2. **Prescribed fire and good fire**. The chances of wildfire escape are rare with one study finding a 0.16% chance of escape across the United States [@escape]. However, even small escapes can create large wildfires and certainly fan the flames of fear regarding fire, regardless [@li]. Some escaped prescribed fires will have benefits. An infamous escaped fire at Caples Lake prior to the 2021 Caldor Fire on the Eldorado National Forest diverted the fast moving fire from South Lake Tahoe and the Tahoe's Western shore where it would have caused significant damage to community and property. **needs cite**.
3. **Protecting senstivie groups**. If even prescribed fire will increase mortality, let's figure out a way to reduce smoke exposure and impact. The good thing about prescribed fire is the smoke is released where and when you want it, unlike wildfires.Early warnings for sensitive groups to avoid smoke exposure, incentives to air filters or even temporary housing elsewhere during burns could pay off for both implementing fires and public health.
5. **Cultural fire**. Tribal nations lived with fire for thousands of years, it's time to put more control of fire back in their hands and co-manage the approach. New efforts are doing this **examples** but more is needed and sharing agency control over resource management and suppression must change more quickly.

# Impacts & trade-offs
1. Firest in strategic locations. Smoke impact may affect human health.
2. High initial investment to ensure there aren't escapes.
3. Restrictive weather, permitting, and capacity limitations may limit scope and frequency of burning
